Overview

Pay science to keep the record of how it is actually done.

Science keeps almost no record of how it's actually done. Null results and failed experiments stay in notebooks. A published paper squeezes months of work into five pages, and its line of reasoning is written after the results are in. Tollens pays researchers for half-hour sittings: an AI reads their raw files, asks only what it can't infer, and produces a record of what was really tried, in what order, and why. AI labs license those records, first as evals their models have never seen and then as training data. After 12 months every record goes public under the researcher's name. Vannevar Bush's Science: The Endless Frontier birthed a system that pays for discoveries and publishes the ones that worked. Tollens pays to capture the real story.

How it works

One record, three ways in, three stops on the way out

The record

One thing, three sources, one rule

Every record is the same thing: the real story of an experiment, with its raw files. What changes is where it comes from and whether its result is already public.

Backlog sittings

A half-hour sitting. The AI reads the files a researcher already has and asks only what it can't infer. About three records per sitting. Stage 1 supply.

Private lab record

The lab logs attempts and mistakes as they happen, inside the lab and its own network first, with scoop protection. A record leaves only when the lab commits it and sets its own release date. Stage 2 supply.

Publication-fee swap

Tollens pays a paper's publication fees in exchange for the full trajectory leading to it: the data, dead ends and reasoning the five pages leave out.

The one rule

Parked: paper path

An unpublished result serves as an eval first, because no model has seen it. A published result goes straight to training, since its answer is already online (my call; parked for you). Everything goes public at 12 months.

Provenance labels

  • Registered beforehand. Reasoning written before the outcome, proven by the timestamp. Highest value.
  • Rebuilt from dated files. The AI drafts from protocols and notebook entries before it sees the outcome.
  • Recalled from memory. Training only.

Hindsight rule

Proposed

The AI drafts each record from dated files before it sees the outcome. The researcher corrects facts but can't rewrite the story. Papers do the opposite: their reasoning is written after the results.

Private lab record: how it works

Proposed
  • Each entry is fingerprinted and timestamped when written. That proves later it wasn't edited, without revealing what it says.
  • Tollens pays the sitting fee when the lab commits a record with a release date. Nothing is paid for entries that never leave the lab.
  • A committed record can serve as a private eval while sealed. The answer key never leaves Tollens; AI labs must agree not to keep eval prompts.
  • Keep it a small attempts log. Benchling owns the full lab notebook.

Evals and the stump count

Decided
  • A public leaderboard with about 5% of items open as samples. Live evals stay private and run through an API on subscription.
  • An item retires on saturation, a serve-count limit or a maximum age, then becomes training data.
  • Each record shows a running count of frontier-model runs it stumped, for example stumped 41 of 46 runs across 9 models, without naming which model failed. The final count is printed on the public record.
  • The rubric and expert follow-up questions stay private when a record goes public.

Biosecurity

Thinking
  • Where recording everything is the norm, hiding work is costly: gaps stand out, and investigators get a timestamped trail.
  • A determined bad actor simply won't record. Claim "harder to hide," never "impossible."
  • The records are dual-use too. Screen anything involving dangerous pathogens or toxins before licensing, with a restricted tier.
  • Who cares: AI-lab safety teams wanting a screened supplier and restricted evals of dangerous capability, biosecurity funders, MATS.

Payout rules

  • Parked The contributor share. The model uses 70% (50 lab, 20 university), but the split waits on the master agreement terms.
  • Decided Logger rule. Records of work up to January 2028 earn the full contributor share. After that, a record made without the Tollens logger loses 20% of its quality score, and so 20% of its royalties. Each lab's clock starts at January 2028 or when it gets the logger, whichever is later, and goes by the date the experiment started. Setups where the logger can't run are exempt. This 20% is the only hindsight penalty, so the provenance label doesn't cut pay a second time. Replaces the 70%/50% rule (Oct 12).

Berkeley agreement

One master agreement fixes four problems at once

Berkeley's IP office (Mike Cohen) supports Tollens. The master agreement is unsigned and its terms are parked for now.

Ownership

Settled fact

UC's Research Data Policy (July 2022) says the Regents own research data. Records with results need the university's licence.

The university's share

Parked

20 royalty points. Royalties versus gifts is unsettled.

Paying researchers directly

Proposed

Paying for time is legal. The data must come through the university, so write direct pay into the master agreement. Faculty report outside work yearly; postdocs need an approved exception. Visa holders: parked.

Overhead on lab money

Parked

Research gifts carry a 13% fee; sponsored research about 60%. A gift tied to submissions counts as sponsored. Options: a flat gift per signed lab, or a fund that pays only after release.

HHMI is the precedent

HHMI employs its investigators directly and signs one collaboration agreement per university, covering lab space, fees and IP, before its first investigator starts. It refuses overhead on its gifts. Copy the structure now; ask for the no-overhead waiver once Tollens brings real money to campus.

Avoid the Innovation Access Agreement

Proposed

Berkeley's standard startup deal lets it buy up to 2% of every raise, SAFEs included, and its licence can't be passed on to AI labs. Ask for royalty points instead, and pay every university the same way so Tollens stays neutral.

Vision

Fill the hole in Bush's system

Bush's hole

Science: The Endless Frontier funded discoveries and published the ones that worked. It never paid for the record of how science is done. Tollens fills that hole.

Navier–Stokes

AI agents just found the Navier–Stokes blow-up, still being checked by mathematicians, by extending a strategy two mathematicians had opened. Math has a mechanical checker. Lab science has only the experiment. Tollens bets that the record of what experiments returned, and the reasoning leading to it, is what models need to make leaps in lab science.

Distilling research taste

Researchers at Anthropic, OpenAI and Edison, and the ML researchers in the Doudna lab, call this the data for distilling research taste. The end state is a pocket Einstein for every researcher, compounding at the speed labs can run experiments.

The next jump

Each jump in intelligence so far, from cell to human, opened frontiers the level below could not imagine. The full record of how science is done is what the next jump learns from, and today nobody keeps it.

A market failure, fixed

A null result is a public good nobody funds. AI labs' private demand finally pays for it. That is Tyler's own field.

Parked Subsidizing other near-functional markets.

Safety

Recorded failures make bad actors harder to hide. Hypotheses registered in advance expose mismatches between what was planned and what was reported.

Governance

What Tollens maximizes: progress itself

Advisory board

Decided

Doudna, Schekman and Betzig are natural fits; Eisen on a separate track. The board advises only. Members' labs may contribute and get paid, so they step out of anything touching payouts or pricing, and you keep the vision.

Commitments live in the contracts

  • The contributor share, cut 20% only for avoidable hindsight
  • Public release by month 24 at the latest
  • Integrity checks free for bioRxiv and journals

Keep Eisen and Schekman apart

They fell out publicly over eLife and don't yet know about each other's support. Ask each before naming them together, and never pitch Schekman or openRxiv in eLife terms.

Keep the audiences apart

openRxiv dislikes content flowing to AI labs. Lead with integrity and open release there; keep the AI-lab story for buyers and investors.
